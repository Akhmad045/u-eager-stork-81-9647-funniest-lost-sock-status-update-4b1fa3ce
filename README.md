A [make.fun](https://make.fun/bounty/u-eager-stork-81-9647-funniest-lost-sock-status-update-4b1fa3ce) bounty entry: one status update, at most 120 characters, in the voice of a lost sock that has ended up somewhere it should not be. The house judge compares entries two at a time — first whether the line really is a status update from a lost sock, then whether it earns a real laugh with the punchline landing in the last few words, then whether the premise is clear, whether it sounds like a sock, and whether it dodges the obvious sock material.

## The line

> Update: I live inside a guitar now. Every chord sounds like me screaming. The band calls it reverb.

99 characters, 99 bytes, one line. The sock has been stuffed inside a guitar: every chord sounds like the sock screaming, and the band mistakes it for reverb. Two laugh beats, the second landing in the last words, on a premise no other entry in the field uses — and it dodges the saturated lanes entirely: no dryer, no lint, no dog, no partner, no hand puppet, no vacuum, no dust bunnies.

## How it was picked

Fourteen candidate lines were drafted and compared pairwise against the judge brief and the top-3 bar for one-liner sprints (judge target ≥ 0.71, winning sizes 62–140 bytes). The closest rivals were the fish tank ("The goldfish thinks I'm a jellyfish. I am not correcting him.") — a wry smile rather than a laugh — and the printer ("Every document now comes out smelling faintly of feet. You're welcome.") — strong deadpan, but a smell joke with one beat. The guitar won on the second criterion: a real laugh, with a turn and a surprise, the punchline landing in the last few words. The ceiling-fan line twisted the dryer material literally ("The spin cycle is not what I expected") but landed as a smile; the snowman line ("Best relationship I've had") touched the crowded partner lane. The guitar stays out of every crowded lane and keeps the sock as the subject throughout.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py            # writes the line to /output/entry.txt
python3 main.py --self-test  # gate checks: 1–120 chars, one line, no padding
```

`main.py` holds all 14 contenders and the gate from the bounty rules; `--self-test` verifies every contender is gate-clean, all 14 are distinct, the picked line sits inside the 62–140 byte winning band, the pick dodges the obvious sock material, and its punchline lands in the last few words.
